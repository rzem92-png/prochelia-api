# PROCHÉLIA — pilot KPI, evidence and GO/NO-GO scorecard (V1)
Date: 2026-11-09
Status: proposed measurement framework. Thresholds must be calibrated before cohort recruitment and approved by accountable humans.

## 1. Measurement rules

- Pre-register the hypothesis, cohort, service/geography scope, measurement window and thresholds.
- Separate actual observed data, estimates, self-reports and model-generated hypotheses.
- Report denominators, sample size, missingness and confidence intervals where meaningful.
- Never label an unobserved metric as zero or success.
- Segment by country, service, provider cohort, customer cohort and acquisition channel.
- Do not optimize GMV, registrations or match rate in isolation.

## 2. Three-sided outcome scorecard

### Client
- qualified request completion;
- time to first suitable response;
- quote clarity and price variance;
- on-time arrival and service completion;
- repeat use and complaint rate;
- satisfaction and accessibility;
- safety incidents and resolution time.

### Intervenant / provider
- onboarding completion and time;
- time to first qualified lead;
- time to first completed mission;
- time from completion to reconciled payout;
- net earnings after platform fees, travel, materials and other known costs;
- earnings per working hour including relevant unpaid travel/admin;
- cancellation/no-show burden;
- repeat clients and retention;
- appeal outcomes and access fairness.

### PROCHÉLIA
- net revenue and contribution margin;
- PSP, refund, chargeback, insurance and support costs;
- customer acquisition cost by channel;
- repeat rate, retention and cohort payback;
- operational cost per completed mission;
- incident/support load;
- platform uptime and workflow failure rate;
- fraud and payment reconciliation exceptions.

### Territory / ecosystem
- new viable provider activities;
- opportunities converted into completed paid work;
- local provider capacity utilization;
- partnerships and repeat demand;
- accessible participation without pay-to-work barriers.

## 3. Proposed pilot thresholds

These are initial hypotheses, not industry benchmarks. Calibrate them before pilot start and document why:
- 100% of bookable service codes have current country/service approval evidence.
- 100% of assigned providers meet the applicable verified eligibility and insurance requirements.
- 100% of payments/payouts are reconciled to authoritative PSP events before the platform marks them settled.
- 0 unresolved critical safeguarding/security incidents at any expansion decision.
- 0 cross-tenant authorization failures in the release test suite.
- At least 90% of pilot missions reach an explicit terminal state with audit trail; exceptions are separately triaged.
- At least 90% of scheduled pilot visits start within the declared service window, excluding agreed customer/provider changes.
- Provider net earnings and customer total are disclosed before acceptance for every paid mission.
- Contribution margin is measured for every pilot cohort; no scaling while the core unit economics are unknown or structurally negative without an approved, capped subsidy.
- Provider and customer qualitative feedback is collected with consent and reviewed before expansion.

Small samples do not prove broad market demand. Report raw counts and uncertainty.

## 4. Hard-stop conditions

Immediately pause new bookings for affected scope if:
- legal/service approval is missing, expired or challenged;
- insurance or required qualification is invalid;
- a serious safeguarding incident lacks an accountable human response;
- unauthorized cross-tenant data access is detected;
- payment/payout ledger and PSP state cannot be reconciled;
- material customer/provider terms or price disclosures are wrong;
- incident response or human support coverage is unavailable;
- a critical security vulnerability is actively exploitable;
- a country pack or service policy is corrupted or unavailable.

Existing missions require a safe continuity plan; pausing new orders must not abandon current beneficiaries or providers.

## 5. Weekly executive review

NEXUS SUPRA prepares a weekly report with:
1. scope and cohort;
2. verified progress against each gate;
3. client/provider/platform scorecard;
4. safety, complaints and appeals;
5. technical reliability and security;
6. financial reconciliation and contribution margin;
7. risks, assumptions and unknowns;
8. proposed actions, costs, reversibility and confidence;
9. decisions requiring founder/authorized human approval.

Every reported metric must link to an authorized source and timestamp. AI-generated summaries are not the source of truth.

## 6. Expansion decision

GO only when:
- every hard gate passes;
- pilot evidence is sufficient for the stated claim;
- client outcomes and provider economics are acceptable;
- the platform has a credible sustainable contribution model or a capped approved subsidy;
- local support and incident capacity are available;
- country/service approvals remain current;
- rollback and suspension procedures have been rehearsed.

Otherwise choose ITERATE, PAUSE or STOP with documented rationale. A GO in France does not imply a GO in Algeria or any other country.
