# PROCHÉLIA — Legal operating framework, France first / international expansion

**Document type:** operational legal-control framework and pre-launch dossier.  
**Status:** drafted 31 October 2026 from the stated PROCHÉLIA project scope and public official sources. It is not a lawyer's legal opinion, a regulatory authorization, an insurance binder, or a certification.  
**Release rule:** no service, territory, payment flow or platform feature may be enabled unless its evidence pack is complete and current.

## 1. Executive decision and legal architecture

PROCHÉLIA is intended to operate as a digital marketplace/intermediary connecting clients with independent service providers for everyday home and accompaniment services. This is a proposed operating model, not a legal conclusion. The legal status follows the real facts: who markets and promises the service, sets prices, selects and directs providers, controls schedules, imposes sanctions, bears performance risk, invoices, receives funds, handles complaints and supplies replacements.

**Target model (subject to French counsel's validation):**
1. PROCHÉLIA contracts with the client for access to and use of the platform, matching, booking workflow, support and any clearly itemized intermediary services.
2. The client and provider enter into a separate service contract directly, only where the service and provider are legally eligible and the arrangement is genuinely independent.
3. PROCHÉLIA is not described as employer, care provider, health provider, home-help provider or guarantor of the provider's performance unless the actual business model, approvals, staffing and insurance deliberately make it one.
4. PROCHÉLIA may not call itself a neutral intermediary while in practice exercising employer-like direction/control, guaranteeing a regulated care service, employing/dispatching staff, or assuming the provider's obligations.
5. The platform's fees, payment role, invoicing, refunds, consumer disclosures and any funds custody must be validated against the actual payment flow and applicable payment-services rules. Use an appropriately authorized payment service provider; do not hold client funds or operate wallets/escrow without specialist advice and a lawful structure.

**Non-negotiable:** contracts, UX, pricing, algorithms, support scripts and day-to-day operations must tell the same story. A clause declaring independence does not cure operational subordination or regulated activity.

## 2. Scope and service boundaries

PROCHÉLIA's proposed catalogue includes accompaniment, errands, household tasks, presence/monitoring and services for older or vulnerable people. These are not one legal category. Each task must be decomposed into its actual acts, beneficiary, location, duration, intervention mode, provider status and risk.

### 2.1 Service classes

| Class | Examples in project | Default launch treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary domestic/life assistance | Cleaning, shopping/errands, household tasks | Conditional; verify SAP scope, consumer terms, insurance, provider competence and exact activity |
| Accompaniment/mobility | Walks, transport, appointments, outings | Restricted pending SAP and transport/insurance classification; do not activate vulnerable-public variants without required approval |
| Assistance with acts of daily living | Personal assistance, dependency-related support | Prohibited by default until legal classification, required agrément/authorization, qualifications, insurance and safeguarding are documented |
| Health/clinical acts | Diagnosis, treatment, medication administration, nursing/clinical acts | Out of scope by default; block unless separately authorized and professionally structured |
| Monitoring/surveillance | Presence, check-ins, welfare visits | Define precisely; never market as emergency response, medical monitoring or continuous protection unless legally and operationally equipped |
| Childcare / vulnerable minors | Any child supervision or accompaniment | Prohibited by default until specific regulatory, safeguarding, qualification and insurance gates are passed |

No service label may be used to disguise a regulated task. The operational task list, not the marketing category, controls classification.

### 2.2 France — SAP decision tree

For each exact service and mode (prestataire, mandataire, mise à disposition), record:
- Is it one of the 26 SAP activities under the current Code du travail / official perimeter?
- Is a declaration needed or desired for tax/social advantages?
- Is State agrément mandatory for the exact activity, beneficiary and mode?
- Is departmental authorization (including the relevant SAD framework / call for projects) required for the exact prestataire activity?
- Does another regulated regime apply (transport, health, social-care, safeguarding, professional qualification)?
- Does the proposed marketplace structure itself qualify as the relevant SAP operator or intermediary? Do not assume a marketplace can claim SAP status or client tax benefits merely because providers perform household services.

**France official baseline checked 31 October 2026:** the government portal distinguishes declaration, agrément and departmental authorization. It lists five activities requiring agrément in specified vulnerable-public / modes and three specified prestataire activities subject to authorization. The authorization process is not a generic NOVA self-declaration; official information describes departmental calls for projects. Each activity must be assessed individually.

- https://www.servicesalapersonne.gouv.fr/je-suis-un-organisme-de-sap/les-obligations-reglementaires-des-organismes-de-services-la-personne

**SAP hard block:** no tax-credit claim, SAP badge, SAP invoice statement, or regulated vulnerable-person service until the entity's exact status, territory, activity, mode and supporting evidence have been checked and recorded.

## 3. Provider independence and employment reclassification safeguards

The independent-provider model is valid only if it reflects reality. Before launch, map and test:
- freedom to accept or decline each mission without penalty;
- freedom to set availability and work for other clients/platforms;
- no mandatory shifts or exclusivity unless legally justified and reviewed;
- no unilateral disciplinary system disguised as quality scoring;
- no automatic suspension based solely on opaque algorithmic scores;
- no continuous GPS outside the strictly necessary mission window;
- proportionate, transparent, purpose-limited verification and safety checks;
- provider ability to negotiate/understand price and contractual scope, where required;
- no hidden control over how the provider performs the service beyond lawful safety/quality requirements;
- actual substitution rights, if promised, and their practical limits;
- clear responsibility for tools, travel, tax, social contributions and insurance, consistent with law.

Safety checks and vulnerable-person safeguards remain essential; they must be designed proportionately and with counsel, not removed merely to simulate independence.

### 3.1 Product features requiring prior legal review
Dynamic pricing, ranking, automated dispatch, availability scoring, acceptance rates, response-time targets, provider performance dashboards, GPS, route tracking, bonuses, penalties, cancellation rules, deactivation, dispute outcomes and AI recommendations may affect status and algorithmic-management obligations. Each feature needs a documented purpose, data basis, impact assessment, human review and appeal path.

### 3.2 Platform Work Directive
Directive (EU) 2024/2831 sets a transposition deadline of 2 December 2026. The exact French implementing legislation and its effective dates must be checked against the Journal officiel at launch and before the deadline. Build for transparency, human oversight, explanation, contestability and data-protection safeguards now. Do not assume either that France has already transposed the directive or that a missed deadline removes existing national duties.

- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/FR/ALL/?uri=CELEX:32024L2831

## 4. Contract stack — required legal documents

The following contracts and policies are separate, versioned, accepted with timestamp and immutable evidence of the version accepted. Do not combine everything into one generic disclaimer.

### A. Client ↔ PROCHÉLIA: Platform Terms of Use
Must identify the legal entity, registration/contact details, platform role, service boundaries, fees, booking process, cancellation, support, complaints, mediation where applicable, consumer withdrawal rules and exceptions, digital functionality, reviews, liability boundaries that are lawful, privacy links, governing law and dispute venue. Do not exclude mandatory consumer rights or liability that cannot legally be excluded.

### B. Client ↔ Provider: Individual service agreement / mission order
Must identify parties, exact task, excluded acts, location, date/time, price, expenses, instructions provided by client, access/safety requirements, cancellation, completion evidence, incidents, payment, insurance evidence, complaint route and applicable legal status. The agreement must not falsely imply the client is employer or provider is independent if actual facts indicate otherwise.

### C. Provider ↔ PROCHÉLIA: Provider Platform Agreement
Must cover platform access, provider eligibility, truthful information, qualifications, insurance, legal authorizations, taxes/social obligations, autonomy, pricing/fees/commission, payment provider, prohibited conduct, safety, safeguarding, documents, privacy, algorithmic tools, human review/appeals, complaints, suspension criteria, evidence retention, termination and dispute resolution. Avoid blanket indemnities or unilateral clauses likely to be unfair/ineffective.

### D. Platform Privacy Notice and Provider Privacy Notice
Separate notices for clients, providers, visitors and, where relevant, representatives/relatives. Identify controllers/processors, purposes, legal bases, data categories, recipients, retention, rights, international transfers, security, cookies/trackers, location data, identity checks, automated decision-making and contact/DPO details where applicable.

### E. Service-specific schedules
Separate schedules for ordinary domestic services, accompaniment/transport, vulnerable persons, child-related services and any future regulated category. Each schedule must list allowed acts, prohibited acts, qualifications, approval evidence, emergency procedure, insurance and escalation path.

### F. Policies and procedures
- Safeguarding / abuse, neglect, exploitation and vulnerable-person incident response.
- Emergency and serious incident escalation (never imply emergency services are replaced).
- Provider identity, qualification, authorization and insurance verification.
- Complaints, mediation and dispute handling.
- Cancellation, refunds, chargebacks and payment reconciliation.
- Reviews, ranking, moderation and appeals.
- Anti-discrimination, accessibility and reasonable accommodation.
- Data breach, retention, deletion and rights requests.
- AI governance, human oversight and contestability.
- Business continuity, backup, restore and crisis communications.
- Subcontractor and supplier due diligence.
- Country launch and change-control policy.

## 5. Responsibility allocation matrix

Allocation is an internal operating rule, not a way to contract out statutory duties.

| Risk / obligation | PROCHÉLIA control | Provider control | Client control | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Platform security, access, availability | Primary for platform systems | Account security | Account security | logs, tests, incident records |
| Accuracy of provider credentials | Verify evidence and status; no guarantee beyond checks actually performed | Supply truthful/current evidence | Report concerns | verification audit |
| Service execution | Define scope and monitor platform process; do not direct beyond lawful model | Perform only agreed lawful task | Provide accurate instructions/access | mission record |
| Regulated activity | Block catalogue and matching absent evidence | Hold personal eligibility/qualification | Do not request prohibited acts | approval register |
| Professional conduct / negligence | Complaint routing and platform duties | Responsible for own acts to extent lawfully applicable | Provide safe environment and truthful information | incident evidence |
| Client safety / safeguarding | Reporting, escalation, safe platform design | Duty under applicable law and contract | Notify risks and emergencies | safeguarding logs |
| Payment and refunds | Own platform/PSP integration and disclosures as contracted | Verify payout details and tax obligations | Pay agreed amount | PSP events, ledger |
| Data protection | Own platform processing and controller duties where applicable | Own independent processing where applicable | Own duties for personal data they control | ROPA, DPIA, notices |
| Employment status | Ensure platform design/operations align with chosen model | Actual independence and obligations | Avoid unlawful direction if direct employer not intended | operational audit |
| Insurance | Insure platform exposures and verify required provider cover | Maintain activity-specific cover | Maintain household/vehicle cover where relevant | certificates and policy wording |

Do not state that a provider alone is responsible for every incident. PROCHÉLIA retains its own statutory, contractual, product, data, consumer and platform responsibilities.

## 6. Insurance architecture and evidence requirements

Obtain written broker/insurer confirmation that policy wording covers the actual marketplace activity, all territories, all service classes, vulnerable-person exposure and the exact contractual model. A certificate alone may not show exclusions.

### PROCHÉLIA company cover — broker assessment required
- Professional indemnity / errors and omissions for platform/intermediary activities.
- Public / general liability for platform-organized operations and bodily injury/property damage exposure.
- Cyber, privacy breach, incident response, ransomware, business interruption and restoration.
- Directors' and officers' liability (D&O), where appropriate.
- Legal expenses / regulatory investigation cover, where available.
- Employer liability / workplace cover if the company employs staff.
- Specific cover for any service it actually supplies or controls.

### Provider cover — verify by service, territory and vehicle
- Professional civil liability covering the exact service and vulnerable-person activities where relevant.
- Personal accident / occupational cover as appropriate to provider status.
- Vehicle motor insurance explicitly covering business/professional use and passenger transport where relevant.
- Tools/equipment and property damage cover where relevant.
- Any legally required sector-specific cover.

### Insurance gate
For each policy capture insurer, policyholder, insured activity, territorial scope, limits, deductibles, dates, exclusions, subcontractor/independent provider wording, vulnerable-person exclusions, transport exclusions, claims-made/occurrence basis, retroactive date, notification deadlines and proof of premium/current status. Set expiry reminders and automatically block new matching for expired or insufficient cover. A platform disclaimer does not replace insurance.

## 7. Consumer, commercial, tax and payment controls

Before launch, determine which rules apply to PROCHÉLIA's actual role and each customer journey:
- consumer pre-contract information, total price, fees, cancellation, withdrawal rights and exceptions;
- unfair terms, complaint handling and mandatory mediation where applicable;
- clear identification of the trader/provider and who is contractually responsible;
- reviews, rankings, sponsored placement and price personalization transparency;
- invoicing, VAT, commission, platform reporting and provider tax obligations;
- DAC7/platform reporting scope, seller/provider due diligence and reporting;
- DSA intermediary classification, notice/action, trader traceability and transparency duties where applicable;
- payment services / PSD2 perimeter, safeguarding of funds and PSP responsibilities;
- chargebacks, refunds, reconciliation, idempotency and financial audit trail.

Do not assume DAC7, DSA, VAT or payment rules apply identically to every feature. Record a reasoned applicability decision with current statutory references and facts.

## 8. Privacy, security and AI governance

PROCHÉLIA may process identity documents, location, home addresses, mission details and information about vulnerable people. These data are high-impact even when not all are special-category health data.

Required controls:
- data inventory and processing records; separate controller/processor roles by processing;
- data minimization and purpose limitation; no collection of health details unless strictly necessary and lawfully justified;
- DPIA screening, and DPIA before high-risk processing where required;
- privacy by design, encryption, least privilege, MFA for administrators, audit logs and tested incident response;
- precise location only during justified mission windows; explain and limit access/retention;
- secure document storage, malware scanning, access logs and deletion schedules;
- processor contracts, subprocessor list, transfer impact assessments and lawful transfer mechanisms;
- rights workflow and breach notification playbook;
- human review and appeal for significant automated decisions;
- AI must not autonomously decide legal eligibility, clinical/safety suitability, hiring/employment status, provider suspension, refunds, payouts, contractual acceptance or deletion of evidence;
- AI outputs must be labelled as assistance, grounded in approved policies, logged, explainable and reversible; sensitive actions require named human approval.

HDS is not assumed automatically. Assess whether the exact hosting/processing arrangement falls within the French health-data hosting regime before storing health data for a relevant third party.

## 9. Corporate and founder protection

Risk reduction measures (not immunity):
- operate through the correctly formed and registered company, with correct activity descriptions and governance;
- separate company and personal accounts/assets; no informal mixing or undocumented withdrawals;
- sign contracts in the company's name and capacity; use approved delegation and signature authority;
- maintain board/management decisions, registers, accounting, tax filings and solvency monitoring;
- do not give personal guarantees or personal undertakings without independent advice;
- avoid personal representations that exceed company authority;
- maintain suitable insurance and timely claims notification;
- document risk decisions, incident response, compliance reviews and remediation;
- do not conceal incidents, misstate provider status, evade tax/social contributions or ignore regulatory orders.

Limited liability is not a shield against personal wrongdoing, personal guarantees, statutory director duties, fraud, criminal conduct, or personal fault where law provides. No wording can guarantee that the founder's personal liability will never be engaged.

## 10. International expansion — mandatory country dossier

There is no single global contract or universal insurance policy that makes PROCHÉLIA compliant worldwide. Launch country by country and, where relevant, state/province/region by region.

For every territory, produce and approve a Country Launch Dossier:
1. local entity/branch, registrations, licensing and foreign investment restrictions;
2. classification of every service and whether home care, social care, health, transport, childcare or safeguarding rules apply;
3. platform/intermediary/employer/agency tests and worker classification;
4. provider onboarding, background checks, qualifications and right-to-work rules;
5. local consumer, e-commerce, marketplace and advertising disclosures;
6. local tax, VAT/sales tax, invoicing, withholding and platform reporting;
7. payment services, currency, PSP licensing and funds-flow design;
8. privacy law, local data residency, transfers, cookies, biometrics and location rules;
9. insurance availability, mandatory limits, territory and exclusions;
10. accessibility, language, emergency, complaints, mediation and dispute rules;
11. sanctions, anti-bribery, AML/KYC and export controls where relevant;
12. local counsel opinion, broker confirmation, regulatory evidence and launch approval.

**No country switch in production** until the dossier is signed off by local qualified counsel and insurer/broker and all approvals are uploaded and independently checked. Country launch must be feature-flagged and reversible.

## 11. Governance and change control

Create a Legal & Risk Register with one record per service, country, feature and supplier:
- unique ID, legal entity, jurisdiction, service/task, beneficiary, intervention mode;
- legal classification and statutory sources with version/effective date;
- permit/approval/registration number, issuer, territory, scope, expiry;
- contract version and acceptance evidence;
- provider qualifications and insurance evidence;
- privacy/DPIA and security controls;
- accountable owner, reviewer, decision date, next review date;
- unresolved risks, compensating controls and explicit launch decision.

Review triggers: law change, new territory, new service, new provider category, payment flow change, new AI model/use, GPS/ranking/dispatch change, serious incident, insurer wording change, regulator request or material complaint.

## 13. What must never be claimed

Do not claim:
- “zero liability”, “fully legally protected”, “100% compliant worldwide”;
- that PROCHÉLIA is only an intermediary solely because its contract says so;
- that every provider is independent solely because they are micro-entrepreneurs;
- that every home service is SAP or qualifies for tax credits;
- that a provider's insurance automatically covers the platform or every activity;
- that an AI system can replace professional legal, clinical, safeguarding or regulatory decisions;
- that a country is approved because another country has been approved.
