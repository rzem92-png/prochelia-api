# PROCHÉLIA — Intermediary Liability Control Standard
**Version 1.0 — 31 October 2026**  
**Scope:** all business activities, services, products, people, suppliers, AI systems and territories.  
**Status:** operational design standard; not a legal opinion, regulatory approval, insurance binder or guarantee of immunity.

## 0. Non-negotiable truth

The objective is to prevent PROCHÉLIA from unintentionally becoming the service provider, employer, regulated care operator, transport operator, payment institution or guarantor of provider performance; prevent foreseeable harm; and ensure every duty that PROCHÉLIA actually has is assigned, evidenced, monitored and tested.

**It is impossible to guarantee that PROCHÉLIA will never be liable.** PROCHÉLIA remains accountable for its own acts and omissions and for mandatory duties that cannot be excluded by contract. Clauses such as “PROCHÉLIA is never responsible” are not a valid liability-control strategy. No disclaimer can legalize an unlicensed service, cure negligent platform design, remove consumer rights, waive data-protection duties, or prevent a regulator/court from examining the actual facts.

The only robust strategy is: choose a lawful model, make actual operations match it, block activities outside it, insure the residual risks, retain evidence, and stop/review when facts change.

## 1. Legal perimeter: determine role activity by activity

PROCHÉLIA must not use one global “intermediary” label for all functions. For every user journey, identify separately whether PROCHÉLIA acts as:
- operator of a digital interface / hosting provider for user content;
- marketplace / trader intermediation service;
- commercial agent or mandatary;
- payment facilitator or technical provider through an authorized PSP;
- verifier of provider identity/qualifications/insurance;
- advertiser or ranking service;
- contracting party to platform access;
- principal supplier of a home service;
- employer or manager of workers;
- regulated social-care, health, transport or childcare operator.

The answer can differ by feature, transaction, country and service. The actual business practices, not the title in the terms, determine legal treatment.

### Mandatory role map
For each service and transaction document:
1. Who advertises and describes the service?
2. Who makes the offer and accepts the order?
3. Who chooses the provider?
4. Who determines the scope, methods, schedule and price?
5. Who can reject, cancel, replace or discipline?
6. Who contracts with the client for the actual service?
7. Who invoices, receives, holds, splits and refunds money?
8. Who bears non-performance, damage and injury risk?
9. Who receives complaints and decides remedies?
10. Who collects and controls personal data?
11. Who owns the tools, uniforms, routes and customer relationship?
12. Who can suspend access, and on what objective/legal grounds?

Any answer inconsistent with the declared marketplace role triggers a mandatory legal hold.

## 2. Employment and provider independence

French law presumes independent status for certain registered persons, but the presumption can be rebutted if actual performance establishes permanent legal subordination. The core test examines authority to issue orders/directives, monitor execution and sanction breaches; an organized service can be an indicator where the platform unilaterally determines execution conditions.

Official case-law reference: Cour de cassation, Soc., 4 March 2020, no. 19-13.316. The Court identifies imposed routes, tariff corrections, inability genuinely to choose work, and disconnection/sanctions as relevant facts in that case: https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/juri/id/JURITEXT000042025162

The 25 June 2025 Commercial Chamber judgment no. 23-22.430 also analyses actual constraints, bonuses linked to work volume/time slots, imposed commercial conditions and restrictions on developing personal clientele: https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/juri/id/JURITEXT000051823947

### Product and operations controls
- Providers choose their availability and may stop using the platform.
- Each mission offer clearly shows task, place, duration, price/fees, relevant client needs and material conditions before acceptance.
- Providers can freely accept or refuse individual offers; refusal must not reduce ranking, access, pay, future offers or trigger hidden friction.
- No minimum acceptance rate, refusal rate, working hours, fixed shifts, exclusivity or guaranteed volume.
- No bonuses that indirectly compel work at prescribed times or quantities without specific legal review.
- Providers may work for competitors and build their own customer base, subject to proportionate privacy and anti-fraud protections.
- No mandatory route or method except lawful, task-specific safety constraints; distinguish safety information from control of work.
- No unilateral price changes after acceptance. Price-setting/negotiation rights must be real and accurately reflected in product behavior.
- No performance scoring or customer rating used as disguised discipline.
- No automated deactivation for refusals, acceptance rates, customer ratings or opaque algorithmic outcomes.
- Complaints are investigated individually, with evidence, proportional interim safeguards, human decision and appeal. A safety restriction is not a disciplinary tool.
- GPS is off by default outside an accepted mission; mission-time tracking is limited to a documented necessity, visible to provider, with restricted access and short retention.
- No mandatory uniform, scripts, continuous availability or managerial instructions inconsistent with independence.
- Quarterly review of actual logs, support practices, product experiments and provider interviews to detect divergence between contract and reality.

If the business needs PROCHÉLIA to direct, schedule, discipline or guarantee provider performance, stop calling it a pure intermediary. Reassess a lawful employment or licensed-service-provider model before enabling those features.

## 3. Marketplace and digital-intermediary duties

Intermediary or hosting protections are conditional and activity-specific, not blanket immunity. A platform can have direct duties for trader traceability, transparency, notices/action, consumer information, content moderation, security, data protection, tax reporting, accessibility, payments and its own contractual promises.

For every content/transaction feature document:
- whether PROCHÉLIA stores or transmits third-party information;
- whether it edits, promotes, ranks, selects or materially controls the content;
- who is the trader/provider and how identity is verified;
- notice-and-action channels and response deadlines;
- reasons and appeal for content or account restrictions;
- advertising/sponsored placement disclosures;
- ranking parameters and main factors where disclosure is required;
- records of trader traceability and verification;
- country-specific Digital Services Act applicability and thresholds.

Never promise “hosting immunity” for a service where PROCHÉLIA is actively determining the underlying service or transaction.

## 4. Contract and funds separation

Use distinct documents and roles:
1. Client ↔ PROCHÉLIA: platform access, matching, platform fees, support and platform-specific promises.
2. Client ↔ Provider: actual service agreement, only if that is the genuine lawful model.
3. Provider ↔ PROCHÉLIA: platform access, commission, verification, data, conduct and dispute procedures.
4. Separate schedules per activity, beneficiary, intervention mode and country.
5. Privacy notices and consent/acknowledgement records by data subject and purpose.

The live product must clearly identify the actual service provider, contract party, price, platform fee, cancellation/refund responsibility, complaint route and applicable consumer rights before commitment.

**Payments:** use a properly authorized PSP for payment execution, split settlements and payouts where required. Obtain specialist confirmation that PROCHÉLIA's actual role does not constitute an unlicensed payment service. Do not hold client funds, create stored-value wallets, escrow, deposits or balances without a validated legal structure. Keep platform commission, provider service price, taxes and refunds separately traceable. Never imply PROCHÉLIA supplies the service if it does not.

## 5. Catalogue and regulated-service firewall

Every service code is blocked until its exact acts, beneficiary, setting, mode and territory have been classified. A broad label such as “accompagnement” or “aide à domicile” is insufficient.

For each service, register:
- allowed acts and prohibited acts;
- intended beneficiaries and vulnerability profile;
- home/outside/vehicle/remote setting;
- SAP declaration, agrément, departmental authorization or other licence where applicable;
- provider qualification, registration, right-to-work and checks;
- insurance policy wording, activities, limits, territory, exclusions and expiry;
- client instructions, home safety and emergency plan;
- safeguarding and incident escalation;
- legal reviewer, evidence source, review date and expiry.

Default BLOCKED pending specific approval:
- clinical, medical, nursing or diagnostic acts;
- medication administration/management;
- personal care or dependency assistance where authorization/qualification is required but not proven;
- passenger transport or vehicle services without appropriate licensing and business-use/passenger cover;
- childcare/minor services without specific regulatory, qualification and safeguarding clearance;
- emergency monitoring, rescue guarantees or medical surveillance;
- any act outside provider qualification, authorization or insurer wording.

No SAP tax-credit claim, SAP badge or invoice statement unless PROCHÉLIA's precise legal status, activity, intervention mode and required declaration/approval support it.

## 6. Verification and safeguarding

Verification must be accurate, proportionate and described honestly. “Identity checked” is not “fully vetted,” “safe,” “qualified” or “guaranteed.”

Record source, scope, checker, date, result, expiry, revocation check, limitations and appeal. Use lawful verification providers. Do not collect criminal-record data unless legally authorized and necessary. Minimize identity-document storage and restrict access.

Safeguarding:
- define abuse, neglect, exploitation, coercion, theft, harassment, unsafe home and missing-person escalation;
- immediate danger: direct users to emergency services (112/15/17/18 as locally appropriate); platform support is not emergency response;
- preserve evidence lawfully, restrict access, avoid amateur investigations and notify competent authorities where legally required;
- provide safe complaint channels for clients, providers, relatives and witnesses;
- human-led triage, documented decisions, no AI-only safeguarding decisions;
- train relevant staff and test scenarios regularly.

## 7. Insurance and claims controls

### Company insurance
Broker must confirm in writing cover for actual platform activities, countries, contractual role, vulnerable-person exposures, verification activities, payment-related exposures and any services PROCHÉLIA itself performs:
- professional indemnity / errors and omissions;
- public/general liability;
- cyber/privacy incident, ransomware and business interruption;
- D&O where appropriate;
- legal expenses/regulatory investigation where available;
- employer/workplace cover if staff are employed;
- any sector-specific cover.

### Provider insurance
Verify the actual policy wording, not just a certificate:
- exact services and beneficiary classes;
- territory and geographic limits;
- professional liability and bodily injury/property damage;
- transport/passenger/business use where relevant;
- vulnerable-person exclusions;
- subcontractor/independent-provider coverage;
- limits, deductibles, claims-made/occurrence, retroactive date and notification deadlines;
- dates and premium/current status.

Automatic controls: alerts before expiry; block new eligible matching if cover is expired, revoked, insufficient or mismatched; log the block and provider appeal. Never state provider cover automatically insures PROCHÉLIA.

### Claims
One claims workflow: immediate safety triage, insurer notification within policy deadlines, preservation of records, legal hold, no admission/denial by untrained support, no deletion, no retaliation, factual communications, and tracked corrective actions. Never promise that a claim is covered until insurer confirms.

## 8. Consumer, tax, payments and commercial integrity

Before each launch, assess applicability and implement:
- pre-contract information, total price, fees, cancellation and withdrawal rights/exceptions;
- unfair terms review, complaints and consumer mediation where required;
- clear trader identity and allocation of service obligations;
- truthful advertising, reviews, ranking and personalized pricing disclosures;
- invoices, VAT, commission, platform reporting and provider tax status;
- DAC7 scope and due diligence/reporting where applicable;
- DSA obligations based on actual service and size;
- payment-services perimeter and PSP contract;
- refunds, chargebacks, reconciliation, idempotency and ledger immutability;
- anti-fraud, sanctions and AML/KYC where legally applicable.

Do not assume all regimes apply identically to every feature. Record a dated applicability decision with legal source and factual assumptions.

## 9. Privacy, cybersecurity and data governance

Data may include home addresses, identity, location, mission details, household circumstances and potentially health or vulnerability information. These are high-impact.

Controls:
- map each processing purpose and controller/processor role;
- data minimization and lawful basis; no sensitive health data unless necessary and lawful;
- DPIA screening and DPIA before high-risk processing when required;
- separate client/provider/relative privacy notices;
- least privilege, MFA, encryption, secure key management and audit logging;
- private object storage, malware scanning, signed short-lived access, no public identity documents;
- location collection only for defined mission purposes and duration;
- documented retention/deletion/anonymization and legal holds;
- processor contracts, subprocessor inventory and lawful international transfers;
- rights request and breach response playbooks;
- independent penetration test, vulnerability management, SBOM/dependency scanning, secret scanning, secure SDLC, backups and tested restores;
- security incident tabletop exercises and evidence retention.

HDS is conditional: assess exact hosting and processing against the French health-data hosting framework; do not assume all PROCHÉLIA processing is covered or exempt.

## 10. AI Copilot and automated decision governance

Copilot is an assistant, not a legal officer, care professional, insurer, employer or executive. It may draft, summarize, detect anomalies, propose prices or prepare workflows within approved data and permissions.

It must never autonomously:
- decide provider employment status or legal eligibility;
- approve a regulated service or missing permit;
- hire, suspend, deactivate, rank or penalize a provider;
- decide clinical, emergency, safeguarding or vulnerability outcomes;
- accept contracts or change legal terms;
- execute refunds, payouts, bank changes or fund transfers;
- delete evidence, alter retention/legal holds or close a serious incident;
- change production, D1 schema, secrets, security policy or deployment.

All high-impact actions require named human approval, least-privilege authorization, preview/diff, reason, second-person approval for critical financial/security actions, immutable audit record and rollback/recovery. AI must explain evidence and uncertainty, abstain on missing information, cite policy versions, and offer contestability.

## 11. Corporate and founder protection

Risk reduction, not personal immunity:
- operate through correctly formed and registered entity; company accounts and assets separate from personal;
- sign in company name and capacity with documented delegation;
- accurate accounts, taxes, governance, registers, solvency and timely filings;
- no undocumented personal withdrawals or commingling;
- no personal guarantee or surety without independent advice;
- appropriate D&O and other insurance, with exclusions reviewed;
- board/management decisions and risk acceptance recorded;
- no false claims, concealment, deliberate non-compliance or ignoring incidents;
- report and remediate incidents; maintain legal hold and evidence.

Limited liability does not shield personal wrongdoing, personal guarantees, criminal conduct, statutory director duties or personal fault where law provides.

## 12. International expansion

There is no universal worldwide compliance setting. Each country, and sometimes region/state, requires its own signed dossier:
- entity, registration and licensing;
- regulated service classification and permitted scope;
- platform/intermediary/employer/agency classification;
- provider qualifications, background checks and right to work;
- consumer, marketplace, e-commerce and advertising laws;
- tax, VAT/sales tax, withholding and platform reporting;
- payments, PSP, currency and funds flow;
- privacy, data residency, transfers, cookies, biometrics and geolocation;
- insurance availability, compulsory limits, exclusions and territory;
- accessibility, safeguarding, emergency, language, complaints and dispute resolution;
- sanctions/anti-bribery/AML as applicable;
- local counsel written analysis and broker confirmation.

Country feature flags remain OFF until evidence is complete. Never infer approval in one country from another country's approval.

## 13. Governance and evidence

Create one Legal & Risk Register record per service × beneficiary × intervention mode × country × product feature. Each record contains:
- unique ID, legal entity, jurisdiction and owner;
- exact service acts and exclusions;
- legal classification, sources and effective dates;
- approvals, licence numbers, scope, issuer and expiry;
- contract/policy versions and acceptance evidence;
- provider qualification and insurance status;
- privacy/DPIA and security controls;
- tests, audit logs, incident history and unresolved risks;
- reviewer, approver, decision date and next review date.

Re-review on any law change, new service, territory, beneficiary, provider class, payment flow, algorithm, AI model, GPS/ranking/dispatch feature, insurer wording change, serious incident or regulator request.

## 14. Release enforcement: fail closed

A service/feature/country cannot become bookable if any applicable requirement is missing, expired, inconsistent or unverifiable.

Automated preconditions:
1. service legal status APPROVED for exact task and territory;
2. required SAP/agrément/authorization/licence evidence current;
3. provider identity/qualification/registration and insurance current;
4. client/provider/platform contracts current and accepted;
5. company insurance confirmed for platform exposure;
6. consumer disclosures, pricing, cancellation and complaints live;
7. PSP and funds flow legally reviewed;
8. privacy/DPIA/security gates passed;
9. safeguarding and incident workflow active;
10. human approval for any high-risk AI or operational action;
11. monitoring, backup, restore and audit evidence available;
12. release owner signs a dated launch decision.

Unknown = BLOCKED. Missing evidence = BLOCKED. Expired evidence = BLOCKED. AI cannot override a block. Manual override requires named authorized human, documented legal basis, second-person review for critical risks, expiry and audit trail; no override is allowed for missing mandatory authorization or insurance.

## 15. What PROCHÉLIA must never claim

Do not claim “zero liability,” “fully legally protected,” “100% compliant worldwide,” “every provider is independent,” “all services are SAP,” “fully vetted,” “guaranteed safe,” “all claims insured,” or “AI ensures compliance.” Use only precise, evidenced claims.
