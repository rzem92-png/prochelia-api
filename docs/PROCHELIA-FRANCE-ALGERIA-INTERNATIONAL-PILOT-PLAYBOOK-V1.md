# PROCHÉLIA — France, Algeria and international pilot playbook (V1)
Date: 2026-11-09
Status: launch preparation framework. No country, service, payment method or regulated activity is authorized by this document.

## 1. Executive decision

Build one international product core with country-specific operating packs. Run two independently governed market preparations:
- France: first controlled operational pilot, subject to service-by-service legal, insurance, payment, privacy and operational clearance.
- Algeria: parallel discovery and readiness pilot, with local partners and a separately reviewed country pack. Start customer/provider research and non-transactional validation before enabling bookings or money movement.
- Other countries: use the same country-pack template and evidence gates; do not activate by copying France or Algeria configuration.

NEXUS SUPRA coordinates readiness, evidence, risks, dependencies, experiments and reporting. The founder/authorized human decision-makers retain market activation and reserved decisions.

## 2. Pilot thesis and differentiation

The pilot must test the PROCHÉLIA Opportunity Economy, not just whether people can register:
**local need → provider capability → eligible service → viable offer → qualified request → trusted fulfilment → verified outcome → fair provider earnings and sustainable platform economics.**

The differentiating test is whether PROCHÉLIA can help create and organize viable opportunities, support the provider's business development, coordinate repeat/multi-provider services and measure outcomes for clients, providers and the platform.

A listing count, AI demo, waitlist or page views do not prove product-market fit.

## 3. Pilot design principles

- One shared identity, catalogue framework, mission model, trust layer, audit and analytics architecture.
- Separate country/service eligibility, taxes, currencies, invoices, consumer terms, insurance, payment rails, privacy and safeguarding.
- Start with a narrow geography and limited service codes, not nationwide coverage.
- Prioritize services with clear scope, manageable safety profile and locally verified legal status.
- Home services are a first-class domain; regulated care/health tasks remain disabled unless independently authorized and appropriately staffed.
- No provider is promised missions or income.
- No paid visibility or subscription may be required to access earned work.
- Quotes disclose customer total, provider expected net and platform fees.
- No live payments, payouts, refunds or financial custody before PSP, legal, ledger, reconciliation and operational approval.
- No country launch based solely on AI research or translation.

## 4. Market pilot tracks

### Track A — France

**Role:** first controlled operational pilot and validation environment.

Preparation:
1. Select one locality based on provider supply, reachable demand, founder/partner operational capacity and service-specific legal feasibility.
2. Interview at least 15 prospective providers/intervenants, 15 prospective clients and 5 local organizations/partners. Record consent and anonymize analysis.
3. Compare current alternatives, unmet needs, customer acquisition channels and provider economics.
4. Select no more than 2–3 service codes for the initial controlled cohort, only after written local review.
5. Build the provider onboarding and eligibility checklist for each exact service.
6. Confirm the legal marketplace/operator model, provider status, consumer disclosures, contracts, cancellation, insurance, tax/invoicing, privacy and complaint handling with qualified local professionals.
7. Confirm PSP onboarding, KYC/KYB, settlement, refunds, disputes and payout model in sandbox before any live activation.
8. Run supervised test missions with synthetic/test data first; move to real pilot only after the release gate.

Candidate discovery categories (not legal approval): ordinary household cleaning, gardening, non-structural small household tasks and other clearly scoped non-clinical services. Personal assistance, childcare, transport, food handling and regulated care require separate service-specific review and may be excluded from the first pilot.

**Pilot locality:** not preselected in this document. Choose it using a written scoring decision; do not assume a city based on founder residence.

### Track B — Algeria

**Role:** strategic second market, with local discovery and operational validation conducted in parallel; transaction activation follows its own independent gates.

Preparation:
1. Identify a local operating partner/adviser with relevant commercial and regulatory competence.
2. Conduct at least 15 provider/intervenant interviews, 15 customer interviews and 5 SME/association/municipal or ecosystem interviews.
3. Map customer acquisition, trust expectations, provider status, service availability, payment preferences and local competitive alternatives.
4. Select a pilot locality only after demand/supply interviews and partner capability review.
5. Create a separate Algeria service catalogue and legal eligibility matrix; do not reuse France's service permissions.
6. Verify exact business/activity status and eligibility under applicable ANAE/CNRC and sector-specific rules; do not assume auto-entrepreneur status covers every activity.
7. Confirm consumer terms, invoices, tax, insurance, labour/subcontracting model, privacy/data transfers and complaint procedures.
8. Validate local payment acquiring, settlement, provider payouts, KYC/KYB, currency/foreign-exchange and cross-border constraints with an eligible PSP and qualified local advisers.
9. Design French, Modern Standard Arabic and Algerian Darija journeys; native review is required. Validate RTL layout, numerals, dates, address entry and customer support language.
10. Start with a non-transactional prototype/waitlist or supervised manual matching if payment/legal readiness is incomplete. Do not take money or confirm a regulated service prematurely.

Candidate discovery categories are hypotheses only. Each must be checked against the exact activity code, local permissions, insurance and provider status before offers become bookable.

### Track C — International expansion

Create a repeatable country activation factory:
1. Market opportunity and competitor research.
2. Provider/customer/partner interviews.
3. Country and service legal classification.
4. PSP eligibility and settlement review.
5. Privacy, data transfer, consumer and tax review.
6. Service operations, insurance and safeguarding review.
7. Language, currency, locale, timezone and accessibility QA.
8. Country-specific sandbox/E2E tests.
9. Local partner and support readiness.
10. Human approval, limited canary, monitoring and rollback.

Country prioritization score (0–5 per factor, evidence required):
- verified unmet demand and willingness to pay;
- reachable provider supply and qualifications;
- legal clarity for initial service codes;
- PSP and payout availability;
- insurance and operational partner availability;
- customer acquisition cost and organic channel access;
- contribution margin potential;
- language/support readiness;
- data/privacy and cross-border complexity;
- ability to safely support complaints and incidents.

Do not select a market on population size or model-generated TAM alone. Apply a hard veto for unresolved legality, payments, safeguarding or support capacity regardless of total score.

## 5. Pilot stages and gates

### Gate 0 — Research ready
- Interview plans and consent scripts approved.
- Competitor evidence log and pricing/terms observations recorded.
- Local service demand/supply hypothesis documented.
- Named local review owners and partner candidates identified.

### Gate 1 — Country/service legal and operational readiness
- Written service-by-service legal review.
- Provider status, qualifications, insurance and prohibited-task rules defined.
- Consumer terms, cancellations, complaints, tax/invoice treatment reviewed.
- Privacy roles, data map, retention, transfers and incident response reviewed.
- Emergency and safeguarding escalation mapped.
- Country pack signed by responsible reviewers.

### Gate 2 — Technical and payments readiness
- Authenticated client/provider/admin workspaces and tenant isolation tested.
- Mission state machine, quote snapshot, schedule, cancellations and support tested.
- Server-authoritative eligibility and pricing policies enforced.
- PSP sandbox KYC/KYB, charge, webhook, idempotency, refund, dispute, payout and reconciliation tested where supported.
- No browser-supplied payment or qualification evidence trusted.
- Backup/restore, monitoring, alerting, incident response and rollback tested.

### Gate 3 — Supervised canary
- Limited invited cohort and explicit service/geography boundaries.
- Human support coverage and escalation owner available.
- Daily operational review; every incident and complaint triaged.
- Provider earnings, customer total, platform cost and service quality measured.
- Stop switch tested and understood.

### Gate 4 — Expand or pause
Expand only when predeclared safety, quality, fairness and unit-economic thresholds are met over a sufficient sample. Pause new orders when legal approval expires, a serious safeguarding issue occurs, PSP reconciliation fails, insurance is invalid, a critical security issue appears or human support is unavailable.

## 6. Suggested 90-day preparation and pilot cadence

Days 1–15:
- Confirm France and Algeria research owners/partners.
- Select candidate localities using evidence, not assumption.
- Conduct initial interviews and competitor mapping.
- Freeze initial service-code candidates and prohibited-task boundaries.
- Build draft country packs and data maps.

Days 16–30:
- Obtain qualified local review of service/operator models.
- Confirm insurance and PSP feasibility.
- Define provider onboarding, customer disclosures and support procedures.
- Finalize pilot hypotheses, sample sizes and measurable success thresholds.
- Validate prototype journeys in French and Arabic/Darija as applicable.

Days 31–60:
- Implement and test the narrow end-to-end vertical slice in isolated preproduction.
- Run synthetic-data tests and supervised usability sessions.
- Test mission lifecycle, cancellations, replacement, complaints, incident handling and reconciliation.
- Complete privacy/security, accessibility and mobile browser checks.
- Conduct a tabletop exercise for safeguarding, outage and payment mismatch.

Days 61–90:
- If and only if all gates pass, run a limited supervised canary in the approved locality/service scope.
- Review operations daily and economics weekly.
- Publish an internal evidence report; do not expand based on anecdotal success.
- Decide expand / iterate / pause per country independently.

The cadence is a planning target, not a promise that legal approvals or technical readiness will be achieved within 90 days.

## 7. Shared operating dashboard for NEXUS SUPRA

Country/service readiness:
- pack version, owner, approval status, expiry and source evidence;
- legal, PSP, privacy, insurance, operations and QA gate status;
- enabled/disabled service codes and geographies;
- unresolved risks, incidents and release blockers.

Opportunity and marketplace:
- qualified requests, time to provider response, match and fulfilment rates;
- provider activation to first qualified lead;
- time to first completed mission and reconciled payout;
- recurring demand, repeat rate and provider capacity;
- cancellation, no-show, replacement and dispute rates.

Three-sided economics:
- customer subtotal, taxes and total;
- provider gross and expected net, including travel/materials and deductions;
- platform net revenue, PSP fees, refunds, chargebacks, support and insurance costs;
- contribution margin by service, locality and cohort;
- provider net earnings per working hour and customer outcome quality.

Safety and trust:
- expired/missing documents, out-of-scope requests and blocked assignments;
- incident severity, human owner, SLA and closure;
- complaint and appeal outcomes;
- access-control anomalies, privacy events and unresolved audit findings.

SUPRA must show provenance and confidence for each indicator. Missing telemetry must be displayed as unknown, never silently treated as zero or success.

## 8. NEXUS SUPRA role

SUPRA may:
- aggregate authorized metrics and prepare country-readiness reports;
- identify demand/supply gaps and draft opportunity hypotheses;
- prepare interview guides, partner briefs, training and content drafts;
- flag expiring approvals, insurance, qualifications and country-pack sources;
- detect anomalies and prepare incident tickets;
- model unit economics and scenarios using validated inputs;
- prepare release checklists and test plans.

SUPRA may not autonomously:
- declare a service legal or a provider qualified;
- activate a country/service;
- sign contracts or make legal representations;
- set live commission/prices or commit spend;
- charge, refund, transfer or pay out funds;
- make safeguarding findings or impose material sanctions;
- migrate production databases or deploy production releases.

## 9. Exit criteria and reporting

Each country produces a signed pilot report:
- hypothesis and cohort;
- approved service/geography scope;
- evidence sources and sample sizes;
- funnel and fulfilment outcomes;
- client outcomes and complaints;
- provider net earnings and time costs;
- platform contribution margin;
- incidents, appeals and safety findings;
- technical reliability and payment reconciliation;
- unresolved risks;
- recommendation: expand, iterate, pause or stop.

A pilot is successful only if clients receive reliable value, providers earn fairly after costs, the platform has a credible sustainable model and safety/legal obligations are met. Growth alone is not success.
