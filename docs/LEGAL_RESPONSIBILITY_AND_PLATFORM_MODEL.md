# PROCHÉLIA — legal responsibility, platform model and launch controls

**Status:** working risk-control specification; not legal advice, legal opinion, certification, or proof of compliance.  
**Scope:** France first; EU and other countries only after a country-by-country approval.  
**Release rule:** this document creates blockers. It does not authorize launch, contract signature, migration, deployment, or a change in the operating model.

## 1. Non-negotiable principle: no promise of zero liability

No contract, disclaimer, company form, insurance policy, or technical control can guarantee that the founder, company, platform, or another participant will never incur liability. Liability depends on the actual facts, conduct, applicable law, and the harm suffered. Contractual allocation cannot remove mandatory duties or rights of third parties, consumers, workers, or data subjects.

The objective is to:
- prevent foreseeable harm and unlawful activity;
- make the actual role of each actor transparent and consistent with operations;
- keep decisions within authorized scopes;
- preserve evidence of controls, consent, warnings, incidents, and corrective actions;
- insure risks that are insurable;
- obtain written professional legal, tax, insurance, and regulatory validation before launch.

Do not market PROCHÉLIA as "zero liability", "100% legally protected", or as guaranteeing the absence of reclassification.

## 2. Actual operating model must match the contract

PROCHÉLIA's intended model is an intermediation / digital marketplace connecting clients and independent providers. That label is not decisive. The real execution of services is decisive.

Before launch, counsel must map each service and answer:
1. Who contracts with the client for the service?
2. Who chooses whether to offer or accept a mission?
3. Who sets or negotiates the price, scope, schedule and method?
4. Who supplies equipment and bears operating expenses?
5. Who directs the work, checks performance, handles complaints and decides sanctions?
6. Who invoices the client, receives funds, pays the provider and bears non-payment/refund risk?
7. Who is responsible for damage, injury, safeguarding, cancellations and replacement?
8. Is PROCHÉLIA acting as marketplace, agent/mandataire, service provider, employer, payment intermediary, or in different capacities for different services?

The answer must be reflected consistently in user journeys, contracts, invoices, marketing, support scripts, pricing, payment flows, and actual practice. If PROCHÉLIA determines the essential terms and directs/control/sanctions providers in practice, do not assume a contractor clause will protect against employment reclassification.

### Required marketplace safeguards (subject to counsel review)
- Provider is a genuine independent business, appropriately registered and insured for the exact activity.
- Provider can freely decide whether to register, publish availability, accept or reject a specific request, set or negotiate lawful rates where the model requires it, and organize the method of performance.
- No penalty, hidden ranking suppression, deactivation, or loss of access merely for refusing work, declining a mission, or choosing availability; objective fraud, safety, legal eligibility and quality rules must be separately documented and proportionate.
- No exclusivity or artificial dependence; provider can serve other customers and use other platforms.
- No permanent worker-style supervision, mandatory scripts beyond legitimate safety/service specifications, or disciplinary sanctions disguised as quality controls.
- GPS only when necessary, proportionate, transparent, time-limited and disabled outside the relevant mission where possible; never continuous worker surveillance by default.
- Ratings and complaints are evidence for fair review, not automatic punishment. Provide notice, explanation, human review, appeal, and correction.
- Do not describe providers as employees, staff, carers, nurses, or agents unless the legal relationship and qualifications support that description.
- Do not use a micro-enterprise registration or a signed waiver as a substitute for factual independence.

**Stop condition:** if the intended commercial model requires PROCHÉLIA to impose fixed shifts, mandatory acceptance, unilateral prices, continuous monitoring, disciplinary deactivation, or detailed directions over how the work is done, pause the independent marketplace model and obtain advice on employing providers or using a legally appropriate regulated service-provider/mandate model.

## 3. Services at home, vulnerable people and regulated activities

Create a service-by-service legal classification before enabling any catalogue item. Separate, at minimum:
- ordinary domestic tasks and errands;
- companionship and non-medical assistance;
- assistance with essential daily activities for elderly or disabled people;
- personal care / acts involving bodily care;
- nursing, medical, diagnosis, treatment, medication administration or other regulated health activities;
- transport, childcare, food preparation, handling of keys, money, access codes or valuables.

Do not allow a category to be activated solely because it appears in the product catalogue. For each service record:
- exact scope, exclusions, client eligibility and provider qualifications;
- whether it is a service à la personne (SAP), and whether declaration, approval/agrément, authorization, or a particular mode of intervention is required;
- whether it falls within regulated social/medico-social or health activities;
- whether HDS hosting is applicable to any health data in the specific processing arrangement;
- required professional registration, checks, training, insurance and supervision;
- emergency and safeguarding procedure.

Never present PROCHÉLIA as a care provider or healthcare provider unless that status is expressly established and authorized. Prohibit clinical acts and medication handling unless separately validated as lawful, properly qualified, insured and operationally governed. Escalate suspected abuse, neglect, danger, incapacity, or emergency through a counsel-approved safeguarding protocol and emergency services where appropriate.

## 4. Contract pack — separate documents, consistent roles

Counsel must draft/review, not merely copy templates:
- platform terms of use for clients;
- provider/platform marketplace terms;
- client/provider service agreement or clear confirmation of who contracts with whom;
- mandate / agency agreement if the chosen model actually uses a mandate;
- privacy notices for clients, providers, prospects and recipients;
- data processing agreements under GDPR Article 28 where PROCHÉLIA acts as processor;
- joint-controller arrangement under Article 26 where applicable;
- payment services / connected-account terms with the licensed payment service provider;
- cancellation, refund, complaint, mediation and consumer withdrawal information;
- safety charter, safeguarding policy and incident response;
- acceptable use, fraud prevention, account suspension and appeals;
- subcontractor, hosting, security and confidentiality agreements;
- IP, confidentiality, retention, and business continuity clauses.

Contracts must state accurately: scope of service, party responsible for performance, provider autonomy, price and fees, payment flow, taxes/invoicing, insurance, cancellation, claims, complaint route, limits of platform role, emergency limitations, data use, retention, and dispute process. Avoid blanket exclusions of liability or clauses that purport to waive mandatory consumer, worker, data protection, or personal injury rights.

## 5. Corporate and founder protection

Before taking bookings or money:
- choose and validate the legal form, governance, capital, corporate purpose, director powers, and separation of personal and company assets with an accountant/lawyer;
- use company bank/payment accounts only; prohibit mixing personal and business funds;
- document director decisions, approvals, delegations, risk acceptance and conflicts of interest;
- maintain accurate accounting, invoices, tax/VAT treatment, provider statements and platform fee reconciliation;
- do not sign personal guarantees or accept personal contractual liability without independent advice;
- maintain a claims register and preserve evidence;
- purchase and verify insurance based on the actual model: professional/civil liability, cyber, directors' and officers' liability where suitable, legal protection, employer liability if relevant, and any activity-specific cover. Confirm exclusions for vulnerable persons, home access, keys, transport, bodily injury, subcontractors and cyber events in writing.
- maintain renewal dates, coverage limits, territorial scope, deductibles, insured activities, and notification deadlines.

A limited-liability company does not shield a director from every personal exposure, including personal misconduct, statutory duties, guarantees, or criminal conduct. Counsel must explain the founder's actual exposure in the selected structure.

## 6. GDPR, data and AI governance

Before processing live personal data:
- identify controller, joint controllers and processors per processing purpose based on actual decisions, not labels;
- maintain records of processing, lawful bases, purpose limitation, minimization, retention schedule and data-subject rights workflow;
- complete DPIA where processing is likely to create high risk; assess vulnerable people, location tracking, identity documents, profiling, fraud, and any health data;
- do not collect health information unless essential, lawful, proportionate and explicitly governed; assess HDS applicability with specialist counsel;
- execute processor agreements, map subprocessors, international transfers, data locations, access controls and deletion/return duties;
- use encryption in transit and at rest, least privilege, MFA for administrators, key rotation, audit logs, tested backups, breach response and retention/deletion controls;
- do not send identity, health, precise location, payment credentials or sensitive documents to general-purpose AI models unless approved under a documented legal and security assessment.

### Copilot constraints
Copilot is advisory by default. It may draft, classify, explain and propose. It must not autonomously:
- hire, dismiss, suspend, rank or penalize providers;
- decide a person's eligibility for work or access to essential services;
- make medical, safeguarding, legal liability or emergency decisions;
- debit, refund, transfer, pay out or change bank details;
- sign or terminate contracts, submit regulatory filings, delete evidence, or change retention;
- change permissions, secrets, production infrastructure, D1 schema/data or deploy code.

High-impact actions require named human approval, reason, preview, idempotency, policy check, audit trail, and reversible workflow where possible. Prohibit self-approval and approval by the AI itself. Provide human contest and correction routes. Assess EU AI Act applicability and deadlines for each feature before activation.

## 7. Consumer, platform, tax and payments obligations

Obtain a written applicability matrix for:
- French Consumer Code: pre-contractual information, price transparency, distance contracts, withdrawal rights and exceptions, cancellation, complaints, mediation, unfair terms, reviews and rankings;
- DSA: determine whether PROCHÉLIA is an intermediary/hosting service/online platform and identify the applicable duties, including trader traceability where required, notice/action, transparency and redress;
- DAC7: determine whether the platform is a reporting platform operator and which activities/sellers are reportable; build seller due diligence and reporting only if applicable;
- VAT, invoicing, income reporting, platform reporting and record keeping;
- payment services: use a duly authorized PSP for regulated payment services; do not hold client funds or provide payment services independently without a legal basis/authorization; reconcile marketplace flows, refunds, chargebacks and payouts;
- accessibility: applicable French/EU accessibility rules and WCAG/RGAA target, with legal applicability confirmed;
- insurance distribution, transport, childcare, food, and other sector-specific rules where relevant.

## 8. Platform directive — urgent 2 December 2026 milestone

Directive (EU) 2024/2831 on improving working conditions in platform work is due to be transposed by Member States by 2 December 2026. It focuses on the actual facts of direction and control, including automated monitoring and decision systems, not merely the wording of a contract. PROCHÉLIA must obtain a France-specific legal assessment before this date and before activating any work-allocation, ranking, pricing, GPS, account suspension or automated management feature.

## 9. Technical release gates tied to legal risks

CI and release governance must fail closed unless:
- runtime DDL/schema creation is removed from request handling and migrations are reviewed/versioned;
- readiness checks every required table, column, index, trigger, foreign key and migration version;
- RBAC tests prove client/provider/admin isolation and resource ownership;
- authorization tests cover IDOR, privilege escalation, session fixation, CSRF, CORS, rate limiting and account recovery;
- audit events are immutable, access-controlled, time-stamped and attributable;
- payment tests cover signature validation, idempotency, concurrency, duplicate/out-of-order webhooks, refunds, chargebacks, reconciliation and payout authorization in PSP sandbox;
- booking tests cover races, overlapping missions, cancellation, no-show, emergency and provider refusal;
- document uploads enforce type, size, malware scan, private storage, short-lived access and deletion;
- backups are encrypted and independently restored in an isolated test database; integrity, schema, row counts and application smoke tests are evidenced;
- dependency, secret, SAST, DAST, SBOM, license and container/build scans are run and reviewed;
- frontend E2E tests cover client/provider/admin flows on iOS Safari, Android Chrome and desktop;
- incident response, support, monitoring, rollback and disaster recovery are rehearsed;
- legal and insurance sign-offs for the enabled service catalogue are recorded.

No real client data, real payouts, live bookings, or regulated service may be enabled before its corresponding gates are passed.

## 10. Country launch dossier

For every new country, require a signed dossier covering:
- entity, permanent establishment and local director exposure;
- employment/platform-work law and collective agreements;
- self-employment registration and social contributions;
- regulated home-care and safeguarding permissions;
- consumer, marketplace, e-commerce and platform rules;
- VAT, income tax, invoicing and reporting;
- privacy, data residency, transfers and breach notification;
- payment services and PSP availability;
- insurance, liability, claims and dispute resolution;

No cross-border launch by default.

Keep versioned evidence: legal opinions, insurance certificates and exclusions, approved service matrix, signed contract versions, DPIA/records, processor register, security reports, CI run IDs, test reports, restore proof, PSP sandbox reports, incident exercises, and named human approvals.

## 12. France-specific update — SAP and platform-work deadline (checked 31 October 2026)

The official French SAP portal states that the required formality depends on the precise activity, target public and intervention mode. Declaration, State approval (agrément), and departmental authorization are distinct regimes. For certain assistance activities for older or disabled people, approval may be required in mandataire / mise à disposition modes, while authorization rules apply to specified prestataire activities. The official list and conditions must be checked for each service before activation.

Official references:
- https://www.servicesalapersonne.gouv.fr/je-suis-un-organisme-de-sap/organismes-de-services-la-personne-comment-demander-une-declaration-un
- https://www.servicesalapersonne.gouv.fr/tout-savoir-sur-les-services-la-personne/les-26-activites-de-services-la-personne

**PROCHÉLIA implementation consequence:** every catalogue item must have a jurisdiction + intervention-mode + target-population legal classification, with evidence of any required declaration/approval/authorization. The catalogue must remain disabled when classification is missing, an authorization is required but absent, or the provider qualification/insurance does not cover the exact activity. Do not advertise a tax credit or SAP benefit unless the exact legal conditions and applicable mode are satisfied and the required declarations/approvals are in place.

Directive (EU) 2024/2831 has a transposition deadline of 2 December 2026. A French Senate committee report dated 22 June 2026 described the French transposition work as ongoing and discussed a possible later implementation timetable. This is a dated parliamentary account, not proof of the final enacted French law. Counsel must verify the legislation and official status immediately before launch and again before 2 December 2026. Do not treat the EU deadline as evidence that France has already transposed the directive, or that the directive's provisions automatically settle every domestic employment-status question.

Reference:
- https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1794723
- https://www.senat.fr/ (committee report, 22 June 2026; search for platform-work directive transposition)

**Launch hold:** no activation of provider ranking, automated allocation, dynamic provider remuneration, GPS monitoring, account suspension/deactivation, or performance-based incentives until a French platform-work lawyer has reviewed their concrete effects on provider autonomy, employment status, algorithmic management, data protection and appeal rights.
